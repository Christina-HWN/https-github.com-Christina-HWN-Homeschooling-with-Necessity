01 • Framework

Demystifying the hour-and-documentation approach

This page is designed as an educational planning framework, not a legal determination. Use the documentation structure to make learning visible, then compare it against the current Missouri requirements and the student's actual educational plan.

Core academics

Document reading, writing, mathematics, science, social studies, and other academic work embedded in projects rather than treating every subject as a separate worksheet.

Home & farm learning

Capture practical work such as animal care, food preservation, gardening, construction, maintenance, water systems, and household management as documented learning experiences.

Electives & projects

Use deeper projects, research, art, technology, entrepreneurship, environmental studies, and other interests to build a strong elective record and portfolio.

Language Arts

Use the homestead as a reason to read, research, write, communicate, and think critically.

Skills
Reading • vocabulary • grammar • composition • research • communication
Evidence
Research notes • journals • reports • instructions • presentations • reflections

Example: Researching poultry nutrition can lead to source evaluation, note-taking, vocabulary, a written care guide, and a final reflection.

Mathematics

Make quantities, measurement, patterns, data, money, and mathematical reasoning part of the work.

Skills
Arithmetic • algebra • geometry • statistics • ratios • financial math
Evidence
Measurements • formulas • tables • graphs • budgets • calculations

Example: Designing a garden requires area calculations, spacing, unit conversion, material quantities, yield estimates, and data analysis.

Science

Use observation and investigation to understand living systems, matter, energy, Earth, and the environment.

Fields
Biology • chemistry • physics • Earth science • environmental science
Evidence
Observations • hypotheses • experiments • data • diagrams • conclusions

Example: Comparing soil conditions can become an investigation involving variables, sampling, measurements, data tables, research, and a written conclusion.

Social Studies

Connect the homestead to people, places, systems, history, government, geography, and economic decisions.

Fields
History • civics • geography • economics • culture • government
Evidence
Maps • timelines • source notes • essays • interviews • economic analysis

Example: Studying local agriculture can lead to maps, historical research, interviews, market analysis, policy research, and an evidence-based written argument.

Integrated Academic Work

The subjects do not have to live in separate boxes.

A strong project can require reading, mathematical reasoning, scientific investigation, historical context, communication, and problem solving at the same time. Map the specific skills actually demonstrated instead of forcing a project into a subject simply to create a label.

Three things to keep separate

1. Missouri requirements: the legal framework that applies to the student's homeschool. 2. Academic mapping: the subject and skill connections supported by the actual learning. 3. HWN documentation: a practical way to preserve evidence of that learning. This framework helps organize the last two; it does not replace the first.

04 • Four Modular Unit Studies

Build transcript-worthy learning around real projects

Unit 1

Soil, Seeds & Food Systems

Soil science, plant biology, measurement, data collection, food systems, and written reflection.

Unit 2

Animal Science & Stewardship

Animal biology, nutrition, observation, ethics, record keeping, and practical problem solving.

Unit 3

Off-Grid Engineering

Energy, water, systems thinking, electrical concepts, efficiency, diagrams, calculations, and troubleshooting.

Unit 4

Homestead Economics

Budgeting, cost comparisons, production decisions, entrepreneurship, market research, and financial writing.

05 • Portfolio

Keep a clean annual portfolio that can support transcripts and future applications.
