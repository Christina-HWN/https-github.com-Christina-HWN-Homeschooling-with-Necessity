Homeschool Organizations & Support

A carefully organized starting point for families looking for legal information, state advocacy, homeschool communities, research, and practical support.

Start with your state. Missouri families have several different kinds of organizations available. Some focus on legal advocacy, some on statewide support, some are faith-based, some are secular/local, and some provide research rather than family services. No single organization is the Missouri equivalent of every HSLDA function, so HWN lists them by purpose instead of treating them as interchangeable.

National Homeschool Organizations

Legal AdvocacyNational

Home School Legal Defense Association (HSLDA)

National homeschool advocacy organization offering legal support for members, state-law information, legislative advocacy, educational resources, and homeschool group services.

Visit HSLDA →
ResearchNational

National Home Education Research Institute (NHERI)

A research organization and clearinghouse for homeschool studies, statistics, scholarly articles, research summaries, and state-specific homeschool data.

Explore NHERI →
Research + AdvocacyChild-Centered

Coalition for Responsible Home Education (CRHE)

Provides research, guides, policy analysis, and resources focused on the educational experience and well-being of homeschooled children. Its policy positions differ from HSLDA, so families should read its materials directly.

Explore CRHE →

Missouri: Statewide Organizations

These are the most important Missouri-specific organizations to place near the top of the HWN directory.

MissouriNonreligiousStatewide

Families for Home Education (FHE)

Missouri's statewide home-education organization. FHE describes itself as representing home educators without affiliation with a religious or political organization and provides Missouri law information, regional contacts, support-group information, events, and family resources.

Visit FHE Missouri →
MissouriChristianStatewide

Missouri Association of Teaching Christian Homes (MATCH)

A statewide Christian homeschool organization providing support to home educators and support groups, information about state and national laws, educational opportunities, and advocacy for home education.

Visit MATCH →
MissouriParent-Directed

Missouri Homeschool Alliance

A Missouri organization centered on parent-directed, parent-funded education. It offers membership, updates, and a Missouri homeschool community resource for families looking for another statewide connection.

Visit Missouri Homeschool Alliance →

Missouri Official & Legal References

Official GovernmentMissouri

Missouri Revised Statutes

The Missouri Revisor of Statutes is the primary place to read the actual statutory language. Start with RSMo 167.031 and related sections rather than relying only on summaries.

Read RSMo 167.031 →
Official GovernmentMissouri

Missouri Department of Elementary & Secondary Education (DESE)

DESE explains that it does not regulate or monitor home schooling and does not require state registration. Its attendance-law information is useful as an official state reference.

Visit Missouri DESE →
Missouri LawSports + Activities

Missouri RSMo 167.790

Beginning August 28, 2025, Missouri law addresses participation by eligible home, FPE, and virtual-school students in specified public-school or charter-school athletics, fine arts, and related activities.

Read RSMo 167.790 →

Local & Community Support

St. Louis AreaSecular

St. Louis Homeschool Network

A local secular support network for St. Louis-area homeschooling families. It describes its mission as welcoming all families without judgment and provides community events such as park days.

Visit St. Louis Homeschool Network →
Find a GroupMissouri

HSLDA Homeschool Groups Directory

HSLDA maintains a searchable directory of homeschool groups and lists Missouri statewide and local organizations by county. Inclusion is a directory listing, not an endorsement.

Find a Homeschool Group →
HWN's approach to this directory: We will not present every organization as if it has the same philosophy. Families should be able to see who provides legal advocacy, who provides Missouri-specific support, who is faith-based, who is secular/local, and who provides research. That makes this page useful to a much wider range of homeschool families—and gives parents enough context to choose the resources that fit their own family.
